What keeps a kelp forest alive?

Move as kelp. Experience the relationships that shape whether the forest thrives, collapses or recovers.

For educatorsAges 12–1460 minutesGroups of 2–3

Using real-time body tracking and 3D interaction, learners experience how sea otter loss can trigger urchin overgrazing, kelp decline and ecosystem collapse. They then explore how restoration can help the forest recover.

Learning intention

Understand how relationships within a kelp forest influence its ability to thrive, withstand disruption and recover.

Download Learner ActivityDownload Educator Guide
Do

Trace how change moves through a kelp forest, connect its benefits for the ocean and people, and suggest an action that supports both.

Think

Recognise a kelp forest as a connected system where changing one relationship can reshape the whole ecosystem.

Feel

Build a sense of connection, vulnerability and informed hope through the forest’s collapse and recovery.

02 · Investigate Look behind the game15 mins

Play through the lens of a scientist.

Become kelp, move with the forest and experience changing conditions. As the ecosystem responds, investigate:

  • What changes first?
  • Which relationships help the forest remain resilient?
  • What drives the shift from kelp forest to urchin barren?
  • What conditions support recovery?

Pause and make sense of the experience.

  • What did you experience that changed how you think about kelp forests?
  • Which cause-and-effect relationship now feels most important?
  • What are you curious to investigate further?
04 · Create Connect the kelp forest15 mins

How does a healthy kelp forest help both the ocean and people?

In groups of 2–3, use the kelp forest drawing starter in the Learner Activity. Colour the forest and add animals, labels and other details that show how life is connected. Then add:

  • animals and plants that depend on kelp
  • ways kelp forests help people
  • threats that can damage the forest
  • actions that could help it recover

Draw lines between ideas. Label each connection with a phrase such as provides a home for, protects, depends on, damages or helps recover.

Discuss

  1. Which benefit matters most to ocean life?
  2. Which benefit matters most to people?
  3. Could helping people ever harm the forest?
  4. What could we do to support both?

Finish this sentence:
We would __________ because it would help __________ and __________.
